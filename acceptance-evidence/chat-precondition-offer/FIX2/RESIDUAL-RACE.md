The existing conditional append compares the latest non-claim conversation row with the caller's expected row under a scenario advisory lock. A concurrent ordinary append visible at this comparison returns `latest_moved`; choice consent refuses immediately, without retry or unconditional fallback. Conditional writers share that advisory lock. FIX2c also binds a press to the durable answer that offered its choices: an ordinary append before the press, or between press resolution and the append-floor read, refuses even when it preserves the same marked hold revision and digest.

The accepted residual is an ordinary non-conditional writer inserting after the SQL latest-row comparison but before the conditional delegate inserts the consent row, including while the delegate waits for its scenario-row lock. That writer does not take the advisory lock, so the check cannot detect its later insertion and stale pending state can replace a concurrent arrival or decline. `created_at = NOW()` uses transaction-start time; insertion/commit order and timestamp ties cannot establish which carrier the reader will consider newest.

A later DL-reviewed and DL-applied migration would acquire the scenario lock before checking latest, coordinate the complete append window with existing writers, and give new consent rows timestamps strictly beyond the locked prior row while preserving replay timestamps and receipts. FIX2c adds no migration and makes no stronger database guarantee. Local rows exercise ordinary pre-press writes, movement before the append-floor check, and movement visible to the existing conditional comparison; they do not witness the database transaction's residual interleaving.
